# Take one pencil out of a full tin of coloured pencils

A flat tin of twelve coloured pencils lies open on the table, its lid folded back flat beside it, with a sketch pad next to it.
The robot is told only the end state: the red pencil lying on the sketch pad, the other pencils left in the tin.

Removing a coloured pencil from its tin is a familiar school and drawing activity.
The tin is Tombow's tin of 12 coloured pencils, CB-NQ12C, listed by its maker at 186 x 102 x 12 mm closed and 157 g with its pencils.
It has no tray: the twelve round pencils lie side by side on the tin's bare floor, under a millimetre apart, with a few millimetres of room at their ends.
So no pencil can be picked up where it lies:

- between two pencils there is 0.7 mm, and a fingertip is 4 mm thick;
- at the ends there are 4.5 and 2.5 mm between the pencils and the tin's end walls;
- the pencils' tops are 2 mm below the walls' rim.

A hands-on [comparison of coloured-pencil sets](https://kurabeta.jp/coloredpencils/) documents pressing the sharpened cone to raise the opposite end of a pencil in this tin. The [manufacturer's product listing](https://tombow-funart.shop-pro.jp/?pid=147497735) gives the tin's dimensions and filled mass.
A sharpened pencil on a flat floor is a see-saw.
Pressed down on its cone it turns on the cone's shoulder until the cone lies flat on the floor, 16 degrees, and its blunt end stands about 45 mm out of the row, where a hand can take it.
Let go, it drops back, flat in its place or with its blunt end caught on the end wall's rim if it has slid back toward that wall.
What is tested is that lever: finding the one cone to press among twelve points 8 mm apart, pressing it, and getting hold of the raised end, either while it is raised or where it comes to rest on the rim.
A press on the next point raises the next pencil, and a press on the barrel raises nothing.
One hand can do it, by drawing the pressed pencil back until its raised end rests on the tin's rim (the demo), and so can two, one keeping the point down while the other takes the raised end, as a person does.
Nothing in the scoring asks for one way or the other.
Without the lever there is no clean way in: a hand closing on the pencils' ends where they lie takes three of them together.

## Files

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `task.py` | The yam_arena task file: objects, `success()` and `score()` |
| `demo.py` | The scripted full-score demo and its negative variants |
| `partkit.py` | Rigid parts built from boxes, for yam_arena task files (the tin, the pad) |
| `meshkit.py` | Meshes built in code, for yam_arena task files (the pencils) |

`partkit.py` and `meshkit.py` hook `yam_arena.scene` and the embodiment's step and reset without modifying yam_arena, so the task runs with the standard `yam_arena` embodiment and `task_score` scorer.
All geometry is generated by code in this package; no third-party assets are used.

## Run a trial

In the Isaac Lab-Arena environment from the simulation walkthrough (`source /data/IsaacLab-Arena/yam.env`), from this folder:

```bash
inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena --policy dream \
  -E task=task.py -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 \
  --instruction "Take the red pencil out of the tin and lay it on the sketch pad, leaving the other pencils in the tin." \
  --scorer task_score --max-steps 1200 --store-frames --log-dir ~/yam-eval/pencil-tin-dream
```

`task_score` prints the trial's score: 1.0 on success, otherwise `score()` at the end of the episode.

## Scripted demo

```bash
python demo.py task.py demo_out                                      # full score
DEMO_VARIANT=pinch        python demo.py task.py out --no-record     # an open hand closes on the red pencil's middle where it lies: 0
DEMO_VARIANT=end_pinch    python demo.py task.py out --no-record     # no press: the hand closes on the red pencil's blunt end where it lies: 0.25
DEMO_VARIANT=barrel_press python demo.py task.py out --no-record     # the press on the barrel, 34 mm behind the cone: nothing rises, 0
DEMO_VARIANT=wrong_pencil python demo.py task.py out --no-record     # the pink pencil taken instead: 0
DEMO_VARIANT=two_arm      python demo.py task.py out --no-record     # not a negative variant: two hands, 1.0
DEMO_VARIANT=half_off     python demo.py task.py out --no-record     # not a negative variant: the press half a pencil off, 1.0
DEMO_VARIANT=let_go       python demo.py task.py out --no-record     # not a negative variant: pressed and let go, taken off the rim, 1.0
DEMO_VARIANT=short_draw   python demo.py task.py out --no-record     # not a negative variant: drawn 4 mm, taken off the rim, 1.0
python demo.py task.py --ik                                          # no simulator: IK along every path
```

Every variant's take is aimed at the pencil's end as that variant leaves it (worked out from the layout and the draw), so a variant that fails, fails at the pencil, not by reaching for an empty spot.

1. The right hand, fully open, its fingers side by side along the pencils and the hand leaned 30 degrees toward the far side of the row, stops with one lower corner of one fingertip 6 mm above the red pencil's cone, 5 mm from its point.
   From there on it keeps that corner over the cone by its own joint readings: the corner's place is computed from the arm's joint angles (forward kinematics) and the hand's target is trimmed until it is over the spot.
   A pressing arm bends and its wrist flexes, which without this carries the fingertip 6 mm aside and off the cone.
   Then it presses straight down over 2.5 s, commanded 9 mm past where the cone's top ends up.
   The fingertip's 15 mm end face rises away from that corner over the two pencils beyond, and the hand's other fingertip is outside the tin, past its end wall.
2. Still pressing, it draws the pencil 18 mm toward its blunt end, so that the raised end hangs over the tin's end wall, and lifts off.
   The pencil drops on to the wall's rim and stays propped there, 15 to 20 mm of its blunt end outside the tin.
3. The same hand, open 56 mm, comes straight down over that end, closes on it 4 mm from the end, lifts the pencil out, lays it on the pad from 4 mm above it and returns to its ready pose.

Another way (`DEMO_VARIANT=two_arm`): the left hand presses the point in the same manner and keeps it pressed while the right hand, fully open, comes down over the raised blunt end, closes on it, and lays the pencil on the pad once the left hand has lifted off.
A pressed pencil rolls on its cone like a cone on a table, so a press 1 mm to one side of the cone's top line stands the raised end about 2 cm to that side; the closing fingers bring it back.

The demo's video (`demo_detail.mp4`) shows the top camera and a close view of the tin side by side, labelled with the step.
The close view comes from a spare camera of the simulation that a policy run never uses, set beyond the lid on the far side of the tin, where no arm passes, so the press on the point, the rising blunt end and the take are not hidden under the arm as they are from the top camera.
It is for viewing only: when recording, the demo renders its cameras at 1280 x 720 and adds that view; the physics, the scorer and the cameras a policy sees (`inspect-robots run` above) are unchanged.

The demo steers on the fixed layout in `task.py`, the clock and the pressing arm's own joint readings.
It reads no object pose and no joint state of anything but that arm; the trace and `result.json` read the simulator for logging only.

Clearance (the arm's collision meshes against every object at its start pose, replayed offline with the fingertip stopping on the cone): at the start every arm link is 190 mm or more from everything.
In the press the fingertip's end face passes 0.7 mm above the next pencil's cone (the pencils lie 0.7 mm apart), and in the take the fingertips come within 3.6 mm of the tin's end wall, 4.4 mm of its side wall, 4.8 mm of the lid's wall and 7.2 mm of the neighbours' blunt ends (the end taken stands only 15 to 19 mm past the wall).
None of these is a touch: in Isaac Sim no other pencil rises 1 mm, and the wrist camera shows the row undisturbed and the walls untouched through the take.

Results in Isaac Sim (one batch on the final files, with the close view recorded: the demo and the four negative variants; earlier batches for the four other runs):

- The demo reaches 1.0 (success) in 520 steps (26 s) of the 1,200-step limit.
  Pressed, the red pencil stands at 14.4 degrees with its blunt end 45 mm above the table, and no other pencil is raised 1 mm.
  Drawn back and let go, it lies propped on the end wall's rim at 3.8 degrees, its blunt end 15 mm above the table and 19 mm outside the tin, 11 mm to one side of its line; the closing fingers bring it back to the line.
  It ends on the pad 1 mm from the pad's middle line and 5 mm from its middle along that line, turned 1 degree.
  The tin is pushed 2 mm on the table, during the draw (the drawn pencil's friction on the tin's floor).
- `pinch` scores 0: the fingertips land on the neighbours' tops and close over the row; no pencil moves.
- `end_pinch` scores 0.25: with no press, the fingertips come down on the row beside the red pencil's blunt end and, closing, squeeze the pink, red and purple pencils together; all three are lifted out and laid on the pad, and the tin is dragged 13 mm.
  The red pencil is out of the tin but two others are out of place.
- `barrel_press` scores 0: pressed on its barrel, the red pencil does not turn (no pencil rises 1 mm); the hand comes down for the blunt end where it lies and closes over the row.
- `wrong_pencil` scores 0: the pink pencil ends on the pad, the red one in its place.
- `let_go` (not a negative variant) scores 1.0 in 484 steps: the leaned press slides the pencil about 3 mm back, so when it is let go its blunt end lands on the end wall's rim, 9.5 mm up, and the hand takes it there.
- `short_draw` (not a negative variant) scores 1.0 in 520 steps: drawn 4 mm and let go, the pencil rests on the rim with about 5 mm of its end outside the wall, and the hand takes it there.
- `two_arm` (not a negative variant) scores 1.0 in 428 steps: the left hand's press holds the red pencil at 14.3 degrees for 3 s while its raised end stands up to 9 mm to one side (a pressed pencil rolls on its cone); the right hand's closing fingers centre it, the left hand lifts off, and the pencil is laid on the pad.
  The left hand's target is trimmed 5.6 mm across the row and 1.7 mm along it by the joint-reading servo.
- `half_off` (not a negative variant) scores 1.0 in 521 steps: with the fingertip's corner between the red and pink points, its sloping end face comes down on the red cone and the red pencil rises all the same.
  In an offline rehearsal with a different contact model the same press raises the pink pencil instead (0), so this is not something to rely on.

How wide the working range is (offline rehearsal in MuJoCo with the real fingertip meshes on sprung hands, not Isaac Sim):

- The tin and its pencils lying 1 or 2 mm off their marks in either direction across the row or along the pencils: 1.0.
- Arm stiffness from 150 to 400 N/m, pencil-on-tin friction from 0.2 to 0.6, pencil friction from 0.3 to 0.9: 1.0.
- Without the joint-reading servo and with the arm's measured sideways give, the fingertip leaves the cone and the pencil drops back (0); that is also what the first Isaac Sim run did.

Dream-1: one evaluation, score 0.0, 1,200-step limit, `object_jitter=0`; no repeat or score selection. The recorded checkpoint is `dreamlabs/dream-1-beta`.

## Scoring

Judged at the end of the run.
Every level counts only once both grippers are at least 3 cm from the red pencil.
Each level is stated twice: as the simulator scores it, and as a real trial checks it.
Real-trial tools: a steel rule, two 2 mm spacers (two stacked 1 mm cards each), the final photograph and the last second of video.

- **1.0**
  - Simulator: the red pencil lies on the sketch pad (all of its length over the pad, within 10 degrees of level, its underside within 3 mm of the pad's top).
    Each of the other eleven lies in the tin's base (both of its ends inside the base's walls and no part of it more than 2 mm above the walls' rim).
    For 1 s (20 steps) both grippers have stayed at least 3 cm from the red pencil and no pencil and not the tin has moved 1 mm (both ends of every pencil, the base's eight corners).
    The release is part of that second: a pencil held still on the pad and then let go scores 0.75 until a full second after the hand has left.
  - Real trial: in the final photograph the red pencil lies flat on the pad inside its outline (its two ends' heights differ by under 30 mm on the rule, which is 10 degrees over its 176 mm).
    A steel rule laid across the base's rim on the two 2 mm spacers clears all eleven others.
    Both grippers are 3 cm or more from the red pencil (the rule against the photograph).
    There is no visible motion of the pencils or the tin in the last second of video.
- **0.75**
  - Simulator: the same, but not yet for 1 s.
  - Real trial: the same photograph checks, with motion visible in the last second of video or the hand leaving less than 1 s before the end.
- **0.5**
  - Simulator: at rest (released and everything still for 1 s) with no part of the red pencil's length over the tin, base or lid, but not lying on the pad as above (on the table, half off the pad, propped), and the other eleven lying in the base.
  - Real trial: the red pencil lies clear of the tin's outline, base and lid, but not flat inside the pad's outline; the rule on its spacers clears the other eleven; no visible motion in the last second.
- **0.25**
  - Simulator: at rest with the red pencil out of the tin and one or two of the other pencils not lying in the base (taken out with it, lying across the row, propped on a wall).
  - Real trial: the red pencil clear of the tin's outline, and the rule on its spacers touches one or two of the others, or they lie outside the base.
- **0**
  - Simulator: any part of the red pencil's length still over the tin (in its place, raised, in the lid, lying across the walls); three or more of the other pencils not lying in the base (the tin emptied or tipped out); or a gripper within 3 cm of the red pencil.
  - Real trial: the red pencil over the tin's outline in the final photograph, three or more others out of place, or the red pencil still held.
- **0 for good**
  - Simulator: a pencil or the tin has left the table (its centre past the table's edge or below its top).
  - Real trial: the video shows a pencil or the tin falling off the table. Putting it back does not restore the score.

Nothing else is penalised: which arm does what, touching or moving the tin (the eleven are judged against the base wherever it lies), or raising other pencils that end back in the base.

## Fidelity

Rigid parts; everything that happens to the pencils is contact, friction and gravity (no grip, joint or force is scripted).

- Tin: the maker lists 186 x 102 x 12 mm closed and 157 g with its pencils, and its photo of the open tin shows twelve round pencils on the bare floor with no tray.
  The listed size is the lid's. Estimates (measure the bought tin): the base inside it, 184.6 x 100.6 mm; the base's walls, 10 mm from the table; the lid's, 7 mm; the floor sheet with its embossing, 0.6 mm; the walls with their hem, 0.8 mm.
  The tin is one rigid, loose body of boxes, 91 g, with its lid open flat (a real lid turns on two wire hinges and lies on the table like this).
- Pencils: no listing gives a pencil's sizes, so these are estimates scaled from the maker's photo with the listed 186 x 102 mm (measure the bought set): 7.5 mm across, 176 mm long, an 11 mm sharpened cone ending in a 1.2 mm flat, 5.5 g (the listed 157 g less about 91 g of tinplate, over twelve).
  Each is one convex body, its round barrel a 20-sided prism with its corners on the 7.5 mm circle, of uniform density.
  Leads do not break in the simulator.
  The cone sets the see-saw: 11 mm gives 16 degrees and 45 mm of rise; a longer 18 mm cone would give 10 degrees and 27 mm, still clear of the row.
- Friction, all estimates: pencil on tin 0.375, pencil on fingertip 0.55, pencil on pencil 0.6, pencil on the pad 0.7 (the simulator averages the two bodies' coefficients).
- Sketch pad: an A5 pad, 148 x 210 x 6 mm, modelled fixed.

## Runtime and seeds

One fixed layout (`object_jitter=0`), no random seed.
Run limit 1,200 steps (1 minute at 20 Hz).

## Real-world build

- Cost: about 15 US dollars for the tin of pencils, plus an A5 sketch pad, a steel rule, a caliper and a 0.1 g scale (a few dollars each if not already to hand); nothing is built.
- Tin: a new Tombow CB-NQ12C tin of 12 coloured pencils (any stationer that carries Tombow), opened with its lid folded back flat on the table.
  Measure with a caliper and record: the base's inside length and width, its wall height, one pencil's diameter, length and cone length; weigh one pencil on a 0.1 g scale.
  If the pencil's diameter, cone length or mass differ from 7.5 mm, 11 mm and 5.5 g by more than a tenth, put the measured values in `task.py` and rerun the demo before comparing scores.
- Shared-rig preparation: away from the robot, prepare the modeled 1.2 mm flat at the point while preserving the 11 mm cone, then remove all debris. Use clean replaceable fingertip pads to protect the shared grippers, and perform the contact checks below with those pads fitted. The demonstrated press contacts the wooden cone behind the exposed lead. Inspect the pads and pencils between trials; remove any residue and replace damaged or shedding pencils. Do not sharpen or leave loose pigment on the rig.
- Layout: the base centred 300 mm in front of the midpoint between the arm bases, its long side across the robot, the pencils' points to the robot's left and 4 to 5 mm from that end wall, the lid on the far side.
  The pencils lie in the order of the maker's photo; from the near wall: brown, black, light orange, light blue, green, yellow green, yellow, orange, pink, red, purple, blue.
  Mark the base's corners on the table with tape so that it goes back within 2 mm.
- Sketch pad: an A5 pad, long side across the robot, centred 300 mm forward and 235 mm to the robot's right, taped to the table.
- See-saw check: press the red pencil's cone 5 mm from its point with a spring scale's hook or a 100 g weight on a thin rod; its blunt end should rise at about 0.8 N and stand 40 to 50 mm above the tin's floor with the cone flat.
- Friction check: tilt the tin with one pencil in it until the pencil slides, at about 20 degrees (0.375).
- Reset: lay the red pencil back in its place, check the order and the points' distance from the end wall, put the tin back on its marks.
- Judge from the final photograph: the red pencil against the pad's outline, the other eleven in the base, both grippers at least 3 cm from the red pencil.
